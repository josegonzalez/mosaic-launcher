# music

A music player in two parts: `musicd`, a daemon that ships beside the plugin's `plugin.so` and plays outside the launcher, so music goes on while the launcher exits for a game, and the plugin, its front end, which talks to it over a Unix socket (`/tmp/mosaic-music.sock`; its pid is in `/tmp/mosaic-music.pid`). The plugin starts it by its path, in a session of its own, the first time something is played; it logs to `.userdata/<platform>/logs/music.txt`, and exits by itself after 10 minutes stopped. It plays MP3, FLAC and WAV (mono or stereo) through SDL's audio, decoded with the dr_libs decoders in `vendor/dr_libs`, from the `folder` setting's folder on the card (`Music` by default) and its folders. The player screen opens from a Music tile on the quick menu (the `tile` setting) or a game list button (the `button` setting, none by default), or `action:music.open`: it lists the folder's folders, then its tracks, in file name order with numbers by value, each by its title tag (ID3v2.2, 2.3 or 2.4, or ID3v1, in an MP3; a Vorbis comment in a FLAC), or its file name without one, and what's playing with its title, artist, time and cover art. The art is the track's embedded picture (an ID3v2 APIC or PIC frame, a FLAC PICTURE block or a Vorbis comment's METADATA_BLOCK_PICTURE, a JPEG or PNG, the front cover first), else the first of `cover.jpg`, `cover.png` and `folder.jpg` in its folder, matched without case; an embedded picture is written to `/tmp/mosaic-music-art/` (a folder of the user's own, with each file made with `O_EXCL` and `O_NOFOLLOW` and renamed into place, only the playing track's kept) for the launcher to load with `art_load`, and scaled once for where it's drawn. Tags are read on a thread of the plugin's own, one track at a time, so a big folder doesn't hold up a frame: names show until titles come in. Tag files are treated as untrusted: every length is checked against the file, tag or block it's in, text frames and comments are read up to 4 KiB, titles, artists and albums are cut to 255 bytes, pictures over 4 MiB, unsynchronised ID3v2.2 and 2.3 tags over 16 MiB, compressed or encrypted frames and anything past 4096 frames, blocks or comments are skipped, and the text shown is always valid UTF-8 (mended with U+FFFD) without control characters. A plays a track and then the rest of its folder, or opens a folder; B goes back up, and closes it from the music folder; X pauses or plays on; L1 and R1 go to the previous track (or the track's start after 3 seconds) and the next; L2 and R2 seek 10 seconds; SELECT turns shuffle on or off and START steps repeat through off, all and one, both kept as the `shuffle` and `repeat` settings. Y opens the lock screen (also `action:music.lock`): the clock and the track, dim on black, taking every button but power until SELECT and then START within a second. `action:music.toggle` pauses or plays on from an FN button. The lock screen shows the art too, dim, above the clock where there's room, and the artist under the title. Over the game list, quick menu, switcher and other plugin screens a now playing pill with the title and artist shows at the bottom for the `overlay` setting's seconds after a track starts or plays on (5 by default; `off` or `always`). While music plays it holds off auto-sleep (the `awake` setting, on by default). Before a game or pak launches the music pauses and the daemon closes the audio device, so the game can have it, and plays on when the launcher comes back; the `games` setting's `play` keeps it playing through games instead, where the device's audio allows two players. A sleep pauses it (it doesn't play on after waking), a pause closes the device after 10 seconds, and a power-off quits the daemon, by the socket or else by its pid. The daemon saves the queue (with its shuffled order), the track, its place and repeat in `.userdata/<platform>/launcher/music/resume.txt` when it quits or is stopped by its pid, and when a track is played, paused, held for a game or put to sleep, through a temporary file it syncs and renames, and removes it when the music stops at the queue's end; it keeps the file, not the plugin, as it has the queue and is what runs when the device powers off from a game. At the next start with no daemon running, the plugin shows that track paused at its place, on the pill and the player, without starting the daemon; the queue's tracks no longer on the card are dropped, and nothing is shown when the track itself is gone. X or `action:music.toggle` starts the daemon and plays on from there, with the `shuffle` and `repeat` settings as they are then. The `resume` setting (on by default) turns this off. Ogg Vorbis isn't supported: the stb_vorbis decoder has memory-safety reports upstream that were never fixed. From Bramley and MaxUI.
